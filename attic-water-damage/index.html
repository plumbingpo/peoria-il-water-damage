Attic Water Damage: What Peoria Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Peoria, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Near Electrical

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Air Conditioner Drips

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

What We Do

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
