Heavy rainstorms, hurricanes, tropical storms and flash floods can do serious water damage to homes and buildings in Peoria. Gaps in siding, broken glass and roof damage all let wind-blown rain into the building. Insulation, ceiling drywall and the hollow spaces in walls can all end up saturated by a leaking roof. During a flash flood, drains cannot keep up and water ends up in the lowest floors. Peoria Restoration provides emergency storm response: we stop more water from getting in, pump out what is standing and begin restoring the property immediately.

Our storm damage repair covers the whole job, from emergency board-up and tarping that keep more water out, through water extraction, structural drying and rebuilding what was damaged. One problem tends to trigger the next after a storm, as a roof opening admits water, wet ceilings and walls break down, and poor drying leads to mold growth. Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. Our technicians trace where the water traveled using thermal imaging plus moisture meters, so no affected area is overlooked.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. Signs You Need Storm Damage Repair in Peoria

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water pooling in the attic, around the chimney or near skylights
  • Water stains that show up on ceilings or walls during or after a storm
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain
  • Wet insulation in the attic or wall cavities found after bad weather
  • Flooding in lower levels because drainage systems were overwhelmed
  • Sagging or detached gutters that spill rainwater down the siding

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Ice dams form when attics run warm, so check that yours has enough insulation and airflow
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Clean out downspouts and gutters regularly, which lets heavy rainfall drain away as it should
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
