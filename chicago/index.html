What Usually Goes Wrong in Chicago Homes

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. We have ordered these five common Chicago calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. The Peoria Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. For Chicago kitchen jobs, our crew can frequently save the cabinets by pushing dry air in at the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. We extract the water, dry the wood to a safe reading and tell you what we think the source is.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across Chicago.

What to Do Before Our Chicago Crew Arrives

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in Chicago, and what to do instead.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

Do shut off the water, open cabinet doors and closets so air can reach them, and hang up wet fabrics and cushions to drip. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. And do make the call early, so Peoria Restoration can have a crew on the way to Chicago while you do the rest.

How Long Drying Takes in a Chicago Home

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. Carpet can be dry on top while the pad is soaked, and painted drywall can look fine while the back of it is wet. For that reason we rely on meters, not on feel. In Chicago we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.
